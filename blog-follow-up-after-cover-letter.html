How to Follow Up After Sending a Cover Letter (With Templates)

6 min read · Updated May 2025

You've sent your application and heard nothing back. Should you follow up? Almost always, yes — done correctly, following up demonstrates professionalism, enthusiasm, and persistence. Done incorrectly, it can annoy the very people you're trying to impress.

Here's everything you need to know about when, how, and what to say.

Key insight: Recruiters receive hundreds of applications. A well-timed, professional follow-up puts your name back in front of them at a moment when they may be actively reviewing candidates. It's not pushy — it's smart.

When to Follow Up

Timing is everything. Follow up too early and you seem impatient. Follow up too late and the decision may already be made.

  • Standard timeline: Wait 5-7 business days after submitting your application
  • If a deadline was stated: Wait 2-3 days after the application deadline has passed
  • After an interview: Send a thank-you within 24 hours, then follow up on the decision after 5-7 days if you haven't heard
  • Maximum follow-ups: Two — one initial follow-up, one final check-in a week later. After that, move on.

How to Find the Right Person to Contact

If the job posting didn't include contact information, do some detective work. Check LinkedIn for the hiring manager or HR contact at the company. Look for the recruiter who posted the role. If you applied through a company's website, there's often a general HR email you can use.

Address your email to a specific person wherever possible — "Dear Hiring Manager" is a last resort.

Follow-Up Email Templates

Template 1 — Standard Follow-Up (5-7 days after applying)
Subject: Follow-up: [Job Title] Application — [Your Name]
Dear [Hiring Manager's Name], I wanted to follow up on my application for the [Job Title] position, which I submitted on [date]. I remain very interested in this opportunity and in joining [Company Name]. Having researched your team's work on [specific project or product], I'm confident my background in [relevant skill or area] would allow me to contribute meaningfully from day one. Please let me know if you need any additional information. I would welcome the chance to discuss my application further. Best regards, [Your Name] [Phone] | [Email]
Template 2 — After an Interview
Subject: Thank you — [Job Title] Interview
Dear [Interviewer's Name], Thank you for taking the time to speak with me yesterday about the [Job Title] role. I found our conversation about [specific topic discussed] particularly interesting and left even more excited about the opportunity. I'm very keen to join [Company Name] and believe my experience in [specific area] would translate well into the challenges you mentioned. Please don't hesitate to reach out if you have any further questions. I look forward to hearing about the next steps. Best regards, [Your Name]
Template 3 — Final Check-In (if still no response)
Subject: [Job Title] Application — [Your Name]
Dear [Hiring Manager's Name], I'm reaching out one final time regarding my application for the [Job Title] position. I remain genuinely interested in this role and in contributing to [Company Name]. If the position has been filled or my application is no longer being considered, I completely understand — and I'd appreciate any feedback if you're able to share it. Thank you for your time and consideration. Best regards, [Your Name]

What Not to Do

  • Don't follow up the next day — it signals impatience and poor judgment
  • Don't follow up more than twice — after two unanswered emails, the answer is likely no
  • Don't be passive-aggressive — "I haven't heard back despite applying two weeks ago" comes across as entitled
  • Don't call unless you have a specific contact number — cold calling HR is rarely welcome
  • Don't add new information unnecessarily — keep follow-ups brief and focused
